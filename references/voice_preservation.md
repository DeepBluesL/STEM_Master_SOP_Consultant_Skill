# Preserve the applicant's voice

Read a few representative sentences for directness, technical density, rhythm, and personal perspective. Preserve those features while resolving unclear reasoning and grammar. Keep effective sentences intact.

For substantial revision, work in this order:

1. Recover the intended meaning and factual claims.
2. Repair the argument or paragraph order where needed.
3. Improve clarity with familiar, precise vocabulary and active verbs.
4. Offer optional stylistic changes separately when they alter the writer's register.

Keep a short edit explanation distinguishing grammar, clarity, argument, and optional style. For fluent drafts, concentrate on evidence and relationships between ideas. For drafts requiring more language support, accept notes in the applicant's preferred language, recover the content, and express it in clear English.

Use the same fact with clearer agency. For example, given the applicant's confirmed note, “I profiled the pipeline; preprocessing took 40 ms of 100 ms,” a useful revision is: “I profiled the pipeline and found that preprocessing accounted for 40 of its 100 milliseconds.” Add a later optimization result after the applicant supplies it.

Preserve uncertainty that belongs to the applicant's experience: an observed correlation, a tentative explanation, or an emerging interest. Express editorial judgments directly. Preserve technical language such as robustness when it names the actual topic.

Apply the user's prose preferences: affirmative explanations; contrast only when essential; boundary discussions when requested; deletion of sentences used solely to preempt objections. Use these preferences in feedback as well as revisions.
