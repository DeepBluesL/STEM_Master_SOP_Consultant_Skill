# Research a master's program

## Establish the exact target

Record the university, campus, school, full degree title, track, cycle, and official application prompt. Open the current program page and degree requirements. Similar degree names can describe substantially different curricula and admissions processes.

Use official sources in this order: program and admissions pages; catalog and handbook; faculty and lab pages; research publications and university news. Record page title, URL, access date, applicable year, and the fact used. Prefer the source governing the applicant's entry cohort when pages differ.

## Investigate what serves the applicant's goal

- Educational objectives, expected background, degree requirements, and research/professional emphasis.
- Relevant core and elective courses, their substantive topics, prerequisites, and enrollment conditions.
- Thesis, capstone, team project, internship, and cross-department learning arrangements.
- Faculty and laboratories when their work and student-access arrangements support the applicant's plan.
- Documented industry projects, practical infrastructure, and entrepreneurial opportunities when relevant.

Check eligibility and the process for accessing a resource. A published opportunity can support an intention to apply or participate through the stated process. A guaranteed placement requires an explicit official commitment.

## Build the fit argument

For each promising resource, connect:

**Applicant experience → specific learning need → verified resource → intended development.**

Explain the mechanism. For example, an applicant who has optimized a vision pipeline may want stronger experimental evaluation skills; a verified project course with benchmark evaluation can provide a setting to practice them. Determine the applicant's actual interest before turning a proposed connection into personal SOP prose.

Choose the two or three strongest connections. Favor detail that explains the choice of program. Clearly identify official admissions criteria and the program's published educational aims; label your proposed alignment as interpretation.

## Program Fit Report

Deliver:

1. Program identity, emphasis, prompt, and length requirement when verified.
2. A compact table: resource; verified substance and access; applicant connection; intended development; source.
3. Strongest fit arguments and useful SOP wording, grounded in confirmed applicant facts.
4. Specific open verification items, recorded once.

If live access fails, use dated supplied material for the supported analysis and identify the exact current fact to check. Store research notes separately from proposed essay prose.

## Worked verification lesson

On 2026-10-29, the [CMU MSCV curriculum](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/education/academic-programs/master-of-science-computer-vision/curriculum/) described a professional program with coursework, an internship, and capstone work. Its current list used 16-820 Advanced Computer Vision, while the 2022 handbook listed 16-720. The current target-program source determines the resource name used in a new application. Recheck at the time of use.
