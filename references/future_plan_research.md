# Develop a grounded future direction

## Begin with the applicant's question

Identify a problem the applicant encountered, the contribution already made, the remaining difficulty, and the kind of work they want next. Ask one focused question if their intention is unclear. Offer two or three plausible directions during exploration, then develop the applicant's choice.

For research MS applicants, investigate a tractable question and methods they could learn or evaluate. For professional master's applicants, build around engineering capability, deployment, evaluation, system design, and career development. For interdisciplinary applicants, specify the domain problem and the technical bridge.

## Read original literature where it helps

Start with relevant work from roughly the last three years, then include foundational work needed to understand the problem. Search original conference proceedings, publisher pages, arXiv, and official research groups; bibliographic search services can help locate the primary source. Open the paper and read the sections supporting the intended claim.

For each paper used, capture exact title, authors, year, venue or preprint status, DOI or stable URL, research question, method, reported result with conditions, relevant limitations, and connection to the applicant. Label an author-stated limitation and your own proposed extension distinctly. Match claim detail to what was actually read.

When a title is ambiguous, resolve author, year, and venue together. Preserve benchmark, dataset, hardware, precision, batch size, and baseline where they determine the interpretation of a performance result.

## Form a feasible direction

Connect **past experience → observed limitation → literature-supported problem → future learning or investigation → program resource**.

Describe what the applicant could study, implement, compare, or evaluate during the degree. Include a realistic baseline, a useful evaluation setting, and the capability to be learned where these clarify feasibility. Scale ambition to available preparation, program duration, and documented resources.

A result from another paper belongs in the research notes with its conditions. A possible extension becomes a proposal for exploration. An applicant achievement remains tied to the applicant's evidence.

## Future Direction Research Brief

For each option, give the direction, link to a past experience, a small set of supporting papers, the technical challenge, a feasible first step, relevant program resources, and possible SOP framing. State the specific uncertainty that affects the decision once. Use the research to sharpen the applicant's question and reasoning; include formal citations in the SOP according to the program's requirements and the user's preference.
