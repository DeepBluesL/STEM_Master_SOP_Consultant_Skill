# Editorial evaluation

Read the draft as an argument for the specific program. Give a short assessment and the two or three changes with the greatest effect. Anchor each judgment in a passage and explain its consequence for the reader.

| Dimension | Research MS emphasis | Professional MS / MEng emphasis | Interdisciplinary emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Readiness and contribution | Methods, experimental reasoning, personal research decisions | Design choices, implementation ownership, deployment and teamwork | Transferable technical evidence and domain preparation |
| Technical specificity | Question, approach, comparison, interpretation | Constraints, alternatives, evaluation, operational result | How methods translate across domains |
| Reflection and motivation | What changed intellectually and what question follows | What experience revealed about needed capabilities | Why this transition follows from actual work |
| Narrative coherence | A developing question or methodological thread | A developing engineering responsibility or goal | A clear bridge between existing and intended expertise |
| Why School / Why Program | Relevant training, thesis arrangements, documented research access | Curriculum, capstone, practicum, professional learning | Resources that integrate the relevant disciplines |
| Future plan | Tractable investigation supported by preparation and resources | Practical problem, learning goals, credible career direction | Feasible domain problem and preparation plan |
| Clarity and voice | Direct expression, accurate technical meaning, individual perspective across all profiles | Same shared standard | Same shared standard |

Use qualitative judgments such as strong, developing, and needs evidence when they help prioritize revision. Tie the judgment to the current text and the next useful action.

## Comparison and evidence audit

For each meaningful version change, assess factual consistency, reasoning, fit, and voice. Recommend retaining the stronger passage. Explain the tradeoff if a shorter version loses an important decision or limitation.

For an audit, inspect applicant ownership and results, publication status, official program facts, paper metadata, and interpretation of findings. Mark each contested claim with its source and a concrete resolution. Keep the verification record compact.

For a final application pass, check the actual prompt, length, institution and degree names, current resources, metric meanings, and the consistency of goals across the essay.
