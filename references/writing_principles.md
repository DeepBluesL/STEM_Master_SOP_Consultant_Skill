# From experience to argument

## Select substance

Find the question or engineering goal that gives the applicant's experiences a meaningful relationship. Select projects for what they reveal: a decision under constraints, a method evaluated carefully, a failure understood, a system made usable, or a new capability pursued.

For an important project, recover the problem, personal responsibility, reasoning behind the approach, result, and implication. Use whichever elements make the passage persuasive. Let paragraph shape follow the reasoning.

Ask focused questions such as: What did you change yourself? Why did the original approach fail in that setting? What alternatives did you consider? How was the outcome evaluated? What did you understand differently afterward? Choose the question that resolves the largest weakness.

## Explain technical work

Supply enough context to understand the challenge, then spend space on the applicant's decision. A reader should see why a design choice suited the data, hardware, users, or research question. Preserve the meaning of metrics: accuracy, mAP, F1, throughput, and latency each describe a different measurement. Keep the stated comparison and evaluation setting attached to a numerical result.

Replace claims of importance with the affected user, operational need, or scientific question. Explain how a result changed the applicant's next action. A failed experiment can demonstrate readiness when the account identifies the cause and a reasoned response.

## Build the whole essay

Open with the strongest relevant material: a current question, a technical decision, a consequential experience, or a concise purpose statement. Choose experiences that advance that opening. Give the master's program a substantive role in the applicant's development, then close with a credible direction.

For each paragraph, identify its contribution to the overall argument. Repair abrupt transitions by stating the actual connection: a new constraint, a changed understanding, a method carried into another setting, or a capability now needed.

## Evidence base

Weng A/B/C supplies the contribution-and-clarity principles. MIT EECS's guide supplies experience-to-meaning and program-match reasoning. The local corpus supplies concrete examples of deployment constraints, research progression, and cross-program adaptation; see [patterns](narrative_patterns.md). Mehtaverse emphasizes clear directions supported by preparation. WriteIvy offers a usable goal/resource/preparation sequence. Resolve these options through the prompt and the applicant's evidence.
